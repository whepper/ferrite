## 8. Earliest demonstrated divergence and per-case classification

### Classification per case

**All 4 cases: Class 1 — Natural block sequence matches, aligned MDCT matches.**

The prior MDCT discrepancy was a capture-stage/scaling (low-pass) artifact. With aligned stages, MDCT is bit-identical.

### Earliest remaining divergence

MDCT matches exactly. The next downstream stage is quantization (S4) and bit allocation (S5/S6). S6 totals differ significantly (oracle uses 25-35% of real C's frame bits). This indicates a downstream discrepancy in quantization or bit allocation, not in the transform or block-switching stages.

The block-switching state matches (including all energy, attack, IIR state). The MDCT matches (bit-identical after low-pass). Therefore, the S6 discrepancy must originate in stages downstream of MDCT: S4 (quantization) or S5/S6 (bit allocation).

## 9. Remaining uncertainties and whether further capture is justified

1. **S6 root cause not localized**: The S6 discrepancy could be in S4 (quantization) or S5 (bit allocation). Further capture of S4/S5 intermediate values would be needed to localize. This is a separate investigation.

2. **Known short-block blip**: The extract_s6.py docstring documents that SHORT frames are "known short-block blips" where oracle inputs don't match the encoder. This experiment confirms the MDCT is not the cause — the discrepancy is downstream.

3. **Bit reservoir**: Real C's larger frame size may be due to bit reservoir allocation. The oracle's bit reservoir model may differ. This is a potential root cause for the S6 discrepancy.

4. **All 4 cases show the same pattern**: Block-switching matches, MDCT matches, S6 differs. This suggests a systematic downstream issue rather than a case-specific one. But per the task constraints, I do not infer a single shared root cause.
